## Billion-level input feasibility — estimate, not rejection

For target level 1,000,000,000, the exact binary64 growth operand is `1.1999999999999999555910790149937383830547332763671875`. Independent directed direct-power bounds agree on the terminal original quote representation `46 × 10^79181246`. The unrounded price has **79,181,248 decimal digits**, spanning about **19,795,312 base-10,000 block positions**; the level-1-to-target material due is **12,500,000,250,000,000**, within signed int64. The [calculation log](p1_proto/exploratory_billion_feasibility_1.log) contains exact operands and formulas. The resumable Godot fallback separately produced the same terminal quote in 31 bit-work units and about 4.1 ms total, with an observed maximum unit of 187 µs. That is a **single high-level quote**, not a MAX sum or wallet.

The independently reduced MAX-50000 exploratory wallet occupied **all 991 positions 0..990** and serialized its minimal synthetic fixture to 13,195 bytes. Extending this measured density to the billion span at an illustrative 17 bytes per block projects **336,520,304 bytes**. At that illustrative byte cost, occupancy above **4.99%** exceeds a 16 MiB whole-save limit. These are quantitative risk indicators, **not** proof of the billion wallet's actual canonical occupancy or whole-save size: carries can create zero blocks, a different approved exact encoding could alter bytes, and no billion-wallet generator has completed. Do not infer an exponent/level cap or reject a compact old `{m,e}` wallet from this projection.

The measured exploratory MAX-50000 query took 2,383,959 µs for 50,000 visited levels, about 47.68 µs per level. Direct linear scaling to a billion levels is **47,679 seconds**; a 10-second level-1-to-billion target requires **100 million levels per second**. Thus tuning an O(level-count) loop or changing a 32-price window size cannot plausibly close the gap on this host. A viable in-domain route requires a **sublinear, exact sum of individually rounded original prices**, with certified threshold/boundary handling and an independently verified canonical wallet, while preserving exact x1/x10/MAX totals. Grouping by decimal magnitude alone still leaves roughly 79 million groups, so it is not yet a demonstrated solution. If a constructed canonical whole save actually exceeds 16 MiB, report that distinct admission failure with exact bytes; test legal high-start and sparse old-wallet inputs separately without presenting them as a replacement level-1 billion PASS. Both construction/admission and sublinear summation remain **PENDING**.

## Correction: actual frame scheduling and stale receipt

The earlier self-`call_deferred` prototype can drain callbacks within one idle cycle. Its 8,123 callbacks, 2.9 ms callback maximum and 28 us worker-thread cancellation **do not demonstrate a responsive main thread**. The earlier `max_change_probe.gd` and ABA control forcibly reset `starting_revision` in their mutation hook, so their reported invalidations **do not demonstrate that an old query returned `stale`**. Those claims are withdrawn; their raw records remain historical.

The new [true-frame completion](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_2.stdout.log) awaits `process_frame` before each work group: 901 contiguous frame IDs, maximum query work 3,322 us per frame, maximum main-thread heartbeat gap 16,722 us, exact fixed MAX-50000 answer, 14,973,139 us whole-query time. The first budget guard [timed out](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_1.meta.json) after one tiny step per frame; it remains a failed experiment. A [main-thread timer cancellation](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_cancel_1.stdout.log) was requested on frame 9 during work and stopped in 14,010 us, with maximum observed heartbeat gap 16,672 us and no live mutation. This proves one actual frame-boundary protocol observation, not an all-input 4 ms bound or 2-second whole-query PASS.

The [stale receipt control](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_stale_1.stdout.log) changes only the external live wallet/revision after eight price visits. The old query's step returns `stale` before the controller explicitly starts a fresh query; it then visits 50,016 prices and reaches the exact answer with the +1 wallet and no query debit. Its maximum frame work was **4,743 us**, so even this fixed run violates a 4 ms per-frame work target despite a 16,740 us heartbeat maximum. This one revision protocol does not establish complete signature dependencies, equal-value profile replacement, or ABA coverage. The old hook-driven results cannot fill those gaps.

The subsequent [four-revision control](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_stale_aba_2.stdout.log) supplies +3, -5, +2 and an equal-value profile-object replacement in successive scan slices. Each old query returned `stale` after eight prices; only then did its caller start a new query. Net wallet delta returned to zero, final query remained exact, and live state matched the last external replacement. Its maximum frame work was **4,572 us** and heartbeat gap 16,730 us. This validates explicit revision signaling for these fixed mutations; it does **not** demonstrate automatic change detection or complete evolution/material/slot signature coverage.

A guard that forced every phase transition into a separate frame [timed out at 30 seconds](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_stale_aba_3.meta.json), because scan/window transitions are frequent. That failed variant is preserved. The later guard isolates the powers preparation phase only: [retest](p1_proto/exploratory_true_frame_stale_aba_4.stdout.log) had four actual stale receipts, exact final answer, 903 consecutive frame work groups, maximum frame work **3,494 us**, maximum heartbeat gap **16,710 us**, and whole-query time **15,024,124 us**. ## Separate ledger gate

### Validated-value lifecycle experiment

**Repair after dual-review FAIL of `79e1864`:** the original session checked host revision only at save, so an external profile change could still debit its old typed balance. The repaired [source](p1_proto/ledger_lifecycle_probe.gd) binds a transient `HostState` owner (the profile itself remains a Dictionary), records generation and the original whole-profile snapshot, and checks owner identity, generation and wallet snapshot inside the *same synchronous debit/credit entry* before building a replacement profile. Host replacement increments generation; an unannounced in-place wallet or level edit also changes the snapshot. Commit creates a new Dictionary profile and then advances the session generation. A failed import first invalidates the old session binding. [First repair run](p1_proto/exploratory_ledger_host_commit_1.stdout.log) failed only because the alias assertion compared equivalent JSON numeric encodings as identical text; the [corrected run](p1_proto/exploratory_ledger_host_commit_2.stdout.log) passed. The final [endpoint and host run](p1_proto/exploratory_ledger_host_full_1.stdout.log) passed equal-value replacement, wrong-host, in-place edit, failed-import invalidation and no-side-effect carry overflow controls. This is an explicit owner protocol, not a claim that GDScript fields are access-private or that all game dependencies are covered.

The flat-array experiment now accepts both signed-int64 endpoint block positions without sentinel collision. Its `combine` checks a carry from maximum position before computing `position+1`; if the exact result needs a position outside its **experimental v4 int64-position domain**, it returns unsupported and the bound transaction leaves host/session unchanged. An encoded out-of-domain position is rejected on load. This does not make an exponent, wallet or level gameplay cap: old `{m,e}` values beyond this prototype representation and any future larger-position encoding require a separate approved converter. The [1/100/1000/10000 regression](p1_proto/exploratory_sparse_sentinel_regression_1.stdout.log) also completed after the arithmetic change.

The repaired single-process measurement included owner checks and an actual Dictionary replacement: cold parse plus host clone/validation was 28.892 ms, one-block committed debit 54.235 ms, equal 10,000-block committed debit 31.252 ms, and save 22.938 ms in [the final raw run](p1_proto/exploratory_ledger_host_full_1.stdout.log). The previous 14/5/12/16 ms lifecycle numbers below describe the **rejected pre-repair session-only prototype**, not the current submission boundary. Neither version meets the 4 ms public target.

Historical pre-repair source at commit 79e1864 and [its final raw run](p1_proto/exploratory_ledger_lifecycle_4.stdout.log) model four distinct entry points without storing an Object in the profile: (1) parse the untrusted profile JSON and strictly decode the wallet once into a transient session-owned typed value; (2) strictly decode a quote at its own untrusted boundary; (3) compare/debit the two already-validated values, replacing the balance with a new value and incrementing a revision; (4) duplicate the current Dictionary profile and encode the session value at save. The API never accepts an externally constructed typed value or returns its internal packed arrays. The caller must supply a monotonic profile revision for replacement detection; a changed or equal-value replacement with a new revision cannot save using the old session. A bad quote invalidates the prepared quote. Source and cost Dictionary aliases mutated after validation have no effect, duplicate debit and stale quote fail, malformed/negative/forged inputs fail, and ordinary/zero balance both round-trip exactly. `bench_sparse.gd` now treats empty `blocks` as canonical experimental v4 zero; its [1/100/1000/10000 regression](p1_proto/exploratory_sparse_zero_regression_1.stdout.log) completed.

One Windows process measured 10,000-block cold JSON parse **4,847 us** and strict wallet validation **9,588 us** (total **14,435 us**). A one-block quote validated in **35 us**; its hot comparison was **9 us**, but producing the new 10,000-block balance still took **4,919 us**. For a full 10,000-block equal quote, quote validation took **9,632 us**, hot comparison **3,057 us**, and exact subtract/new-value construction **12,015 us**. Cloning the Dictionary profile and encoding its 10,000-block wallet for save took **16,130 us**. These are separated measured components, not an end-to-end 4 ms success; the earlier full untrusted public path still fails. The local session encapsulates mutable packed arrays by API convention; GDScript does not enforce private fields against a caller deliberately accessing its internals. A production interface would need an explicit owner/lifetime boundary and revision discipline, and this audit does not prove the full legacy schema or arbitrary exponent domain.

An additional [full-boundary measurement](p1_proto/exploratory_full_public_ledger_1.stdout.log) on 10,000 consecutive blocks measured **34,111 us** to parse and strictly decode two JSON operands, **3,143 us** for typed compare, **6,999 us** for typed add, **5,952 us** for typed subtract, and **13,853 us** to encode the sum to JSON. The measured sequential public compare path is **37,254 us** and add path **54,963 us**; the latter is not an isolated entire-function timer but includes every measured constituent of the public call. Input was 108,908 bytes; compare and arithmetic round trip were correct. Immutable already-validated typed values could skip repeated parse/decode in one in-memory session, but even typed add was 6,999 us and public load/save boundaries still require full validation and encoding. A design claiming a 4 ms public gate from only typed compare would change the measured boundary. A different representation and explicit trusted-value lifecycle require independent proof; the current GDScript encoding remains failed.

The 10,000-block **public** compare/add/subtract/encode gate remains independent of MAX improvements. The earlier [Godot raw timing](p1_proto/exploratory_sparse_runner_3.stdout.log) observed parse **5,027 µs**, decode **9,980 µs**, compare **2,826 µs**, add **5,816 µs**, subtract **6,096 µs**, and encode **14,033 µs** for one consecutive-block input. The <=4 ms public-operation target includes validation/decoding; optimizing quote generation, caching a query-local ledger, or measuring only the internal compare cannot satisfy it. No repeated runs are needed to re-label these known exploratory misses as PASS. Candidate directions include a more compact validated storage encoding and a fast immutable typed representation or native-backed conversion, subject to exact round-trip and whole-save checks; neither is implemented or approved here. No block-count, decimal-exponent, wallet-value, or gameplay-level limit is introduced by this audit.

## Gate decision

**Limited same-object dual review of `a26a4803a977dba1da1efc2ca010bfe461503afb`:** dot and the original astra both accept the two specified *audit-prototype correctness repairs*: no int64 endpoint sentinel collision or unchecked carry at `position+1`, and host identity/generation/full-profile change checked at the debit submission boundary with failed-import invalidation. They do **not** pass P1 overall, the complete large-number domain, the 4 ms ledger target, or product integration. The experimental v4 position overflow is recorded as an unsupported representation result, not a gameplay exponent or level cap. The owner protocol relies on application ownership; GDScript does not enforce private immutable fields. The extreme-MAX fee/refund choice is still awaiting the user decision; approximate debit remains unauthorized. This record ends the present repair round without starting another algorithm candidate.

The lifecycle is viable as a boundary model, but the current flat-array representation copies all 10,000 blocks even for a one-unit debit. Reusing a validated value removes repeated decode; it does not make the hot new-value path or save boundary meet 4 ms. A persistent sparse representation with structural sharing or a different compact encoding would be a materially different candidate and must first prove canonical zero, exact carry/borrow, round trip, alias isolation, revision invalidation and the *full* public timings in this audit. No new native dependency or product storage type is inferred from this result.

The `ccf23b9` amendment supersedes only the old exact extreme-MAX count and exact billion cumulative-wallet prerequisites. The remaining fee/refund rule is unresolved, and this audit does not claim a new extreme-MAX acceptance test or implementation. Fixed ordinary MAX-50000 and ledger public-cost misses remain evidence of present failures.

P1 remains **NOT PASSED**. The exact 50,000-price comparison and isolated semantic controls strengthen correctness evidence; the 2-second MAX-50000 and 4 ms 10,000-block public ledger gates still fail or lack a full candidate. The level-1 billion wallet is not constructed/admitted, and the final generator, canonical hashes for all fixed scenarios, full native/save consumers, and ten independent processes remain pending. P2/product connection/main merge remain closed until dot and original astra agree on one complete result object.
