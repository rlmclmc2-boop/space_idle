# Physical and energy as the primary weapon distinction

**Review state:** measured direction, not an accepted production balance change or a finalized 18-point opening. Production still uses laser 300/1.5 s, four 200-damage missiles/2.4 s, cannon 1500/3.5 s, and the existing charging beam. The 120-damage missile is a test-only option. The proposed 25%/1.0 s recovering shield is also test-only. All comparisons below use the existing firing, travel, resistance, targeting, and beam rules.

## Actual defense rule and player cue

Laser and beam have damage type 1 (energy); missile and cannon type 2 (physical). An enemy's shield and hull have independent types. Matching either layer's type applies the configured 50% reduction. The shield absorbs `min(current shield, max(1, incoming * (1 - resistance)))`; any unabsorbed incoming continues after accounting for that factor. Hull damage is `max(1, ceil(incoming * (1 - resistance)))` on a matching hull. The old presented executor (`game.gd:2603-2619`) and native kernel (`combat_execution_kernel.h:1018-1050`) follow this rule. The real draft enemy `270007` combines physical-resistant shield type 2 with energy-resistant hull type 1; a neutral-hull fixture cannot stand in for it.

Type already has player-facing cues: orange physical and blue energy weapon/defense shapes and outlines in `enemy_recognition_visual.gd`; the defense inspector explicitly names each layer's resistance, including shield break. This establishes an implemented cue, not that players notice or understand it in play. No new presentation mechanic was added.

## Why the current numeric relation weakens the type axis

At level 1, pulse sustained raw throughput is `300/1.5 = 200/s`; current missiles are `4*200/2.4 = 333.3/s`. Into physical-resistant hull, missiles still deliver about `166.7/s`, 16.7% below pulse's 200/s (equivalently pulse is 20% above missile). Into energy-resistant hull, pulse delivers 100/s against missile's 333.3/s. This asymmetry is broad, before discrete kills: the apparent physical penalty barely changes the missile's relative throughput. With the 120 candidate, missiles deliver 200/s raw, 100/s into physical resistance, versus pulse's 200/s; against energy resistance they deliver 200/s versus pulse's 100/s. These approximately twofold opposing type advantages give a simpler basis for later enemy and weapon authoring than a special 240-300 HP band.

Both equipment rows use 1.2 growth and the same level-1 cost 50 with 1.2 cost growth. Applying the production integer equipment growth over levels 1-100 gives current missile/pulse per-hit damage ratio 0.632-0.706 and candidate ratio 0.379-0.420. Thus the corresponding sustained pulse/missile advantage against physical hull is about 1.13-1.27 current, 1.90-2.20 candidate. The missile/pulse advantage against energy hull is about 3.16-3.53 current, 1.90-2.10 candidate. These are finite same-level formula checks, not universal time-to-clear guarantees: group size, opening, travel, overkill, mixed layers, and slot choice still matter.

## Actual typed targets, 1x

Six physical-resistant hull targets: current missiles sometimes beat pulse **despite resistance**. At 120 HP the old executor times are pulse 9.10 s, current missile 6.47 s; at 180, 9.10/6.47; at 260, 9.10/11.07; at 400, 18.20/13.72. The 120 candidate at those same HP gives missile 6.47, 11.07, 18.32, and 25.57 s respectively. Its low-HP opening exception is consistent with four parallel tubes; across 180-400, pulse leads with a substantial gap. Energy-resistant six-target hull at 260 HP gives pulse 18.20 s, current missile 6.47 s, and candidate missile 11.07 s. Both missile values still win their intended opposite-type target.

With level-4 equipment, two identical slots, six 900-HP targets, and production growth, the physical-resistant case gives pulse 9.10 s, current missile 11.07 s, candidate missile 18.32 s; the energy-resistant case gives pulse 18.20 s, current missile 6.47 s, candidate missile 11.07 s. Native timings differ by at most 0.18 s and preserve all six rankings; native admission succeeded in every one of these six comparisons. This is a focused second equipment/slot sample, not a full progression matrix.

At 1x and nominal `1e40`, physical-resistant 260 HP and energy-resistant 260 HP preserve both executors' above winner relations, for current and candidate missile. The realistic dual-layer recovering target (1100 HP, 600 shield, physical shield and energy hull) gives beam 9.13 s old / 9.15 s native, current missile 11.07/10.98, and candidate missile 28.22/28.02 at 1x. At `1e40`, the candidate missile needs 51 rather than 48 hits and takes 30.52/30.30 s, but beam remains clearly ahead. All 28 current-typed and 32 candidate-typed scale rows completed; the 12 and 16 native runs respectively admitted and did work.

One current-damage edge illustrates why exact HP tuning should not carry the design: six physical-resistant 400-HP targets favor current missile 13.72 versus pulse 18.20 s at 1x, but at nominal `1e40` favor pulse 18.20 versus missile 18.32 s. Four reduced 200-damage hits exactly empty 400 HP at 1x; an extra hit is needed after large-scale subtraction. The user does not require identical boundary hit counts. The practical lesson is to keep the role relation comfortably away from an exact-hit cliff. The candidate's physical-resistant 400-HP pulse lead remains at both scales and in both executors.

## Within-type mechanics and unresolved cost

Physical missile's four early, parallel tubes clear ten 100-HP targets in 6.23 s in the neutral test; physical cannon clears a heavy two-target 5000-HP pair in 28.13 s and uniquely beats the sampled reactive stack window. Energy pulse clears the sampled neutral six-target 280-HP group in 9.10 s; energy beam suppresses the candidate recovering shield by hitting every 0.28 s. These are secondary mechanics and sampled comparisons, not enemy designs that require an exact hit threshold. The separate [neutral scale note](SCALE-ROLES.md) demonstrates finite scaling for those samples; it does not close the type-primary design.

The 120 candidate changes early stage kill frequency and therefore resource pacing. The restored stage-2 layout had 976-HP targets, making that candidate materially slower there. Its production adoption requires an authoritative Excel edit, projected JSON regeneration, and a short serial check of incoming damage, affordable upgrades, and progress rewards. The old/native structural contracts pass, with known small scheduling offsets. No long playthrough or production weapon/enemy parameter change was made in this review. Memory recovery at high enhancement levels and stage-18 resource pressure remain separate open risks.
