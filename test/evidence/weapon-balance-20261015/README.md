## What the focused probes established

The probes run the `legacy_presented` executor only, with fixed seed 1729, production stat cache, one level-1 weapon, no enemy attacks, saves disabled, and a 60 s ceiling. They are mechanics evidence, not a normal playthrough or a native weapon firing-chain test. `role-probe-160-rejected-clamp.jsonl` describes an older 160-damage proposal whose fixed-relative residual clamp was rejected. `role-probe-3-missile-candidate.jsonl` and `pulse-interval-3-missile-candidate.jsonl` describe the later **unapproved** 3 × 200 / 1.4 s candidate; the `raw/` directory retains its original Godot output.

At the 4 × 160 interim proposal, a 5–8-target, 180–620 HP sweep found **no pulse win** at any of the 48 sampled count/HP combinations. With 3 × 200 missiles and the fixed 1.5 s pulse, five, seven, and eight targets at 220–300 HP favored pulse, but **six targets favored missile 8.77 s versus pulse 9.10 s**. At 3 × 200 and pulse 1.4 s, all 5–8 target counts favored pulse in the tested 220–300 HP band; that pulse change violated the fixed baseline and was rolled back. A target in `200 < HP <= 300` needs one 300-damage pulse but two 200-damage missiles; the per-target hit-count advantage is real, yet volley grouping and the missile's fast opening can reverse the whole-group result. One-shot low-HP missiles and high-HP cannon advantages were also measured in the candidate fixture. None of these candidate timings authorizes changing the fixed pulse or missile salvo.

The draft recovering-shield candidates showed why shield strength must be chosen after the weapon baseline: 35%/0.8 s blocked the 3-missile launcher for 60 s, while 25%/1.0 s admitted all four weapons and favored continuous beam. That enemy remains unmounted and its original 8%/1.8 s values have been restored. The original shield with a 1.5 s pulse shows zero shield regeneration against that pulse in the measured fixture, so it does not yet establish a distinct continuous-beam role.

## Numerical precision boundary

The fixed `max_hp × 1e-12` residual clamp was removed: it could erase representable true health. Both direct tests preserve a 50-million-HP remainder after a hit against a module with `1e20` maximum. The legacy direct contract reports **66 checked, 0 ordinary failures, 1 unresolved extreme boundary**; native reports **58 checked, 0 ordinary failures, 1 unresolved extreme boundary**. Both scripts exit nonzero for that open requirement. At `k=1e40`, mathematically exact 25 hits of `200k` into `5000k` leave `8e-13` normalized HP in both executors. The 3-missile candidate role probe at that scale needed two extra hits and 2.17 s. There is no exact high-scale hit-count equivalence claim. [Precision derivation and bounded design option](precision-boundary.md) distinguishes true low-ratio HP, input quantization, and accumulated subtraction error; [isolated DLL source and build hashes](BUILD.md) and `raw/` retain original outputs. Native checks direct hit/defence equations, **not** four-weapon launch, targeting, and projectile travel.

At `k=100` and `100000`, selected candidate first fire/hit, kill time, hit count, applied HP/shield loss, and regeneration matched 1×, but normalized reported overkill did not: roughly 3 for pulse and 0.6 for beam against the recovering fixture. `GrowthNumber.ceiling()` contributes up to one actual damage unit per hit at 1×; its relative effect vanishes at large `k`. No measured kill time changed in those samples, but a boundary target could change outcome. This is a real quantization limit, not merely display rounding.
