# Why the relative residual clamp was removed

The rejected code zeroed enemy HP or shield whenever the remaining value was at most `maximum × 1e-12`. Legacy player modules used full module capacity for that threshold. Native player modules used their **current pre-hit** value instead. Thus the same attack could be lethal in one executor and survivable in the other. It also erased representable, earned health; it was not limited to floating-point subtraction noise.

Concrete counterexample (Python IEEE-754 double calculation, and now covered by both direct Godot tests): original maximum `1e20`, current HP `1e15`, incoming hit `1e15 − 5e7`. The actual remainder is `5e7`. At current HP `1e15`, one ULP is `0.125`, so this remainder is 400 million ULPs and cannot reasonably be dismissed as a rounding trace. The old maximum-based threshold was `1e8` and would kill the actor early. The native player's current-based threshold would be only `1e3` and would preserve it. The error could reach almost `1e8` effective HP for that maximum. More generally, an `H × 1e-12` rule can remove any genuine remainder in `(0,H × 1e-12]`, including a small damage case after high durability has already been depleted.

At `k=1e40`, exact mathematical 25 hits of `200k` against `5000k` left about `8e-13 × k = 8e27` HP in the native floating representation. The rejected `max_hp × 1e-12` threshold was `5e31`, so it would delete even much larger real remainders while masking this case. After removal, the native direct test reports the residue; the actual three-missile heavy-target role probe also needs two extra hits and 2.17 s at this scale. Normal-scale and `k≤100000` exact-25-hit direct cases settled to zero. The supported conclusion is limited: direct normalized equations align on the sampled lower scales, but strict extreme-scale kill timing does not.

`GrowthNumber.ceiling()` uses `ceilf` on ordinary floats. Its +0 to +1 damage quantum per hit does not scale with `k`, so normalized reported damage/overkill can shift even where settled HP and timing remain the same. In the current recovering-shield fixture, pulse reported overkill is about 3 lower and beam 0.6 lower at high scale. This is an actual per-hit damage-amount difference, not merely a display artifact. It did not alter this fixture's effective HP/shield loss or kill time; a target nearer a hit boundary may differ. No local tolerance has been substituted because the stored float alone cannot reliably distinguish a genuine small remainder from accumulated subtraction error.

## Input quantization versus repeated subtraction

For `k=1e40`, let `H` be the actual binary64 value received for `5000*k`, and `D` the actual binary64 value received for `200*k`. Treating those *received values* as exact rationals gives `H − 25D = 2.1663950687494155e27` HP. After 25 ordinary binary64 subtractions the remainder is `9.284550294640352e27` HP. Therefore about `7.118155225890937e27` is accumulated subtraction error, but `2.1663950687494155e27` is already present from independent input quantization. A compensated accumulator can remove the **subtraction error** and still cannot make the received input ratio exactly 25:1. The two floats alone cannot reveal whether the intended source was exactly `5000:200` or a genuinely slightly larger HP value.

The user subsequently set relation stability, rather than exact last-hit equality, as the balance acceptance criterion. Both executors retain this residual as a diagnostic. The formerly considered source-unit shadow ledger is out of scope and has no planned implementation; a blanket residual clamp also remains rejected because it erases genuine HP. The [candidate-A five-scale role comparison](SCALE-ROLES.md) tests the requested observable criterion at selected magnitudes while leaving the extreme mathematical discrepancy visible.
