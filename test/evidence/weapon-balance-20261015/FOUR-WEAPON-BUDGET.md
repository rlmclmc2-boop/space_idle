# Four-weapon type-first output budget for review

**Historical first candidate:** the [revision after targeted testing](BUDGET-REVISION.md) supersedes the 1000/3.5 cannon recommendation below.

**Decision state:** formula-based proposal only. No authoritative Excel/JSON or combat rule changed here. The pulse's 1.5 s baseline cooldown is fixed by the user. Other base damage, cooldown, missile count, and same-target salvo policy may be considered. This proposal changes two base damages and simplifies one beam tick interval; it changes no firing or targeting rule. It keeps a four-tube 2.4 s missile launcher and allows multiple tubes to concentrate on one surviving enemy; disabling that concentration could make the last enemy linger. The 18-point opening stays unmounted pending this review and a later economy/pressure check.

## Long-run constraints before encounter geometry

At equal level and one slot, let `P = pulse damage / 1.5 s`, `M = missile count * per-missile damage / launcher cooldown`, `C = cannon damage / cannon cooldown`, and `B = beam damage * maximum ramp multiplier / beam interval`. With 50% matching-type resistance, a broad type-primary budget should at least satisfy `P > M/2` and `P > C/2` into physical-resistant hull, plus `M > B/2` and `C > B/2` into energy-resistant hull. `B > M/2,C/2` against physical resistance and `M,C > P/2` against energy resistance are additional checks. These are sustained-output comparisons, not kill-time promises. A mixed shield/hull can reverse preferred type when a layer breaks. Damage ceiling, travel, target selection, overkill, and charge time remain relevant.

| Same-level level-1 weapon | Current raw / resisted DPS | Three-field proposal raw / resisted DPS | Mechanic retained |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Pulse, energy 300 every 1.5 s | 200 / 100 | unchanged | Early single hit, ordinary repeat. |
| Missile, physical 4 x 200 every 2.4 s | 333.3 / 166.7 | **4 x 170 = 283.3 / 141.7** | Four individually targeted travelling hits; fewer than four enemies cause multiple tubes to share targets. A missile does not reacquire after its target dies. |
| Cannon, physical 1500 every 3.5 s | 428.6 / 214.3 | **1000 = 285.7 / 142.9** | Large spaced hit can avoid short reactive-stack window and reduce heavy-target shot count. |
| Continuous beam, energy 35 each 0.28 s, ramp 1x to 3x | Uncharged rate 125 / 62.5; plateau 375 / 187.5 | **0.25 s tick: uncharged 140 / 70; plateau 420 / 210** | First hit at 1.00 s; then four ticks/s. Multiplier rises by 0.25x per tick from 1x to 3x, reaching cap at 3.00 s if the same beam remains locked. |

Resisted values divide sustained raw throughput by two before per-hit ceiling. At level 1, current physical-resistant cannon still delivers **214.3/s**, above pulse's 200/s; current missile delivers 166.7/s, **16.7% below** pulse (pulse is 20% above missile). Proposed cannon becomes 142.9/s and missile 141.7/s, so pulse is about 40% above either resisted physical weapon. Into energy-resistant hull, proposed missile 283.3/s and cannon 285.7/s each exceed the beam's **fully charged** 210/s by about 35-36%. The energy beam's early ticks are weaker still. This balances the two physical weapons against both energy archetypes without relying on exact enemy HP bands.

Using `ShipDatabase.equipment_growth` including its rounding, a finite same-level check over ordinary levels 1-100 gives the smallest proposed advantages: pulse over resisted cannon **1.333x**, missile over resisted fully charged beam **1.212x**, pulse over resisted missile **1.354x**, and cannon over resisted fully charged beam **1.212x**. The level-1 values differ from those minima. This checks output ordering across a broad level range; it does not cover arbitrary enhancement multipliers, gear combinations, or per-encounter utilization. All four authored rows currently have the same level-1 resource-1 purchase cost 50 and cost growth 1.2, so unequal slot or upgrade budgets require a separate affordability check.

## Why mechanics may still win a particular fight

The type advantage is the default on sufficiently long, comparable targets. A four-tube launcher can use parallel targets and its 0.4 s first salvo to beat an energy weapon on a tiny swarm; that is an encounter geometry result. It must retain enough focus on the last enemy to finish a single tough target in normal time. A cannon hit is large and infrequent, so it can clear reactive stacks or waste most of a hit on low HP; the proposed 1000 damage is still larger than a proposed missile's 170 per tube. A continuous beam first hits at 1.00 s, then hits every 0.25 s: **four ticks per second**. Its multiplier starts at 1x and gains 0.25x of the **base** damage each tick, reaching 3x at the ninth hit, 3.00 s after lock starts; this is additive ramping, not repeated multiplication by 1.25. With base 35, scheduled pre-rounding hits are `35 × [1, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2, 2.25, 2.5, 2.75, 3]`. Each actual hit resets the shield-recovery delay. These mechanics may overturn a short individual fight; they should not make the resisted physical weapon win a long, non-reactive physical-resistant attrition test, or the resisted energy weapon win the corresponding energy-resistant test.

Existing evidence isolates the concern without new Godot runs: the current neutral 5000-HP pair gives cannon 28.13 s, beam 30.00 s, missile 30.40 s; a reactive version gives cannon 28.13 s while beam and missile fail to finish in 60 s. With a test-only 120 missile, the neutral heavy missile time is 49.97 s; a 10 x 100-HP low swarm is cleared in 6.23 s by the same four-tube launcher. Current typed targets and candidate 120 are detailed in [TYPE-AXIS.md](TYPE-AXIS.md). The [targeted old/native result](FOUR-WEAPON-RESULTS.md) now checks early groups, single-survivor focus, and heavy/reactive ranking. It finds a strong type-level direction but only a 0.30 s neutral-heavy cannon lead over missile, so no finished four-weapon balance is claimed. The 170/1000/0.25 values are a coarse output budget, not a tuned one-hit threshold; 120 remains a separately measured candidate, not a required choice.

## Minimum existing interface for Astra's presentation review

The eight unmounted draft rows already have both a stable config `id` and an authored `archetype_id`: `270001/scout`, `270002/sentry` (physical-resistant hull); `270003/interceptor`, `270004/swarm`, `270005/anchor`, `270006/reactive`, `270008/command` (energy-resistant hull); and `270007/shielded` (physical-resistant shield over energy-resistant hull). `270006` also has `reactiveReduction=1`. `270007` currently has shield recovery delay 1.8 s and 8% of maximum shield per second. These row-to-archetype pairs identify the present draft fixtures only. No draft `27000x` row is bound into the current first three stages: they still use the old `41xxx`/`42xxx`/`43xxx` enemy rows, which have no `archetype_id`. Do not bind new art to those legacy IDs by size or assume the draft stage placement is approved.

The established `BattleGame.event(kind,payload)` signal emits `encounter` on group spawn, `fire`, `beam_started`, `beam_hit`, `hit`, `explode`, and `wave_clear` in the old executor; the native session maps its feedback to the corresponding `fire`, `beam_started`, `beam_hit`, `hit`, and `explode` kinds. `hit` for an enemy has `player=false`, `uid`, `type` (1 energy / 2 physical), `amount`, `x`, `y`, and `critical`; reactive hits additionally expose `reactive_stacks` and `reactive_multiplier`. The native adapter includes an `absorbed` key on enemy `hit`, but the kernel currently adds local shield absorption into `amount` without assigning it to the feedback `absorbed` field; the old enemy hit payload does not include that key. It is not a trustworthy split or shield-break interface. `fire` payload shape varies for the native laser fast path, so a presentation consumer should rely on the small common fields (`type`, position) and current visual/read model rather than `shot` existing for every fire. `explode` for an enemy carries its enemy dictionary; distinguish player deaths, which use a separate boss-marked dictionary.

`spawn_group()` duplicates the configured enemy row, retaining its `id` and `archetype_id`, then assigns a fresh instance `uid` and formation `slot`. The native compiler duplicates that row again, and the native kernel retains it as the actor view returned to `game.enemies` after admission. Thus current draft `id`/`archetype_id` are readable on both owner paths; `uid` identifies one spawned instance, while `slot`, `size`, and combat-context `source_id` are not enemy-kind identities. Existing `CombatContext.root_id` and `batch_id` travel inside attack provenance, and `shot.serial` identifies a projectile, but the common `hit` event does not publish attack root/batch or an authoritative event clock. Native feedback contains `clock` and `target_uid` internally, but `native_execution_session.gd` drops `clock` and attack provenance when adapting `hit`. Do not infer a same-round cannon batch, exact reduction reason, or preattack countdown for presentation from near-simultaneous events.

For type/layer presentation, the existing read-only `game.enemies` dictionaries expose `uid`, `armourType`, `shieldType`, `hp`, `max_hp`, `shield`, `max_shield`, `shieldDelay`, `shieldRecovery`, `shield_hit_at`, and `shield_updated_at`. `enemy_defence_inspector.gd` already uses the type and shield-presence fields to label the exposed resistance and broken shield on hover; `enemy_recognition_visual.gd` uses orange physical and blue energy outlines. Shield recovery updates in `settle_enemy_shield()` without a dedicated recovery event. Read current state at presentation refresh; do not infer shield breaking or repair solely from `hit.amount`, and do not require a new combat mechanic for this UI handoff.
